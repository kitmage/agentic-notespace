# Inclusive & Accessible
We strive to create an inclusive and accessible space that is welcoming to all people who have an interest in the Dharma, regardless of race, nationality, gender, orientation, or past or present religion. We do not tolerate hate or discrimination of any kind.

While the internet, and the world in general, can feel like a storm of bad information and toxic attitudes, we hope our community serves as a refuge from both, where we can learn and grow together in a safe and respectful environment.

We will be imperfect in our pursuit, and we hope you will help us improve.

# __Community Rules__

**It is impossible to enforce our way to harmony, and no number of rules or moderators can create a healthy community. A healthy and harmonious community is rooted in the mindfulness and wholesome intentions of all of its members, including you.**

Here are some guidelines for creating the conditions for a thriving community:

## 1. Honor the intention of the Virtual Sangha.
Honor the intention of the server, which is to support and encourage the study and practice of Buddhism through wholesome engagement in good Dharma-friendship.

## 2. Do not encourage breaking the Precepts. 
Do not glorify violence, theft, sexual misconduct, wrong speech, or intoxication. Learn more about the [Five Mindfulness Trainings](https://plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-5-mindfulness-trainings).

## 3. This is not a platform for medical, legal, or financial advice.
Do not create an unsafe environment by offering or seeking medical, legal, or financial advice. In case of a mental health crisis, please use https://virtualsangha.org/helpline

## 4. No hate.
Treat all living beings with dignity. We do not tolerate hate speech or bigotry of any kind.

## 5. Use the correct channels.
Keep conversations in the appropriate channels, such as limiting conversations that might reasonably require a trigger-warning (including discussing the intersections of Buddhadharma and other religions or politics) to the Mud to Lotus Forum.

## 6. No spamming, scamming, or boasting of spiritual authority or attainments.
Do not engage in the activities of charlatans (boasting of spiritual attainments, asserting spiritual authority, spamming, or scamming).

## 7. Respect the Stewards (moderators)
Respect the Stewards, even if you disagree with their handling of any particular issue, as they are servants of the community who volunteer their time and attention to keeping the Sangha safe and healthy. All mod actions (timeouts, kicks, bans) are reversable and are open to discussion in the right time (in the middle of a Steward actively trying to address a problem in the community is usually the wrong time).

## 8. Check yourself instead of checking others.
When, despite our efforts, some difficulty inevitably arises in the Sangha, remember that your response is your responsibility no matter what the other person is doing. Do not speak or act from a place of agitation. Do not be reactive as untrained people tend to be. Recognize your own agitation as an invitation to deepen your practice, showing you where you can develop greater compassion. If you sense that the conditions for fruitful conversation are not present, then it's okay to keep noble silence until conditions for communication have improved. Know when to take a timeout, or open a ticket and let the Stewards take care of the situation.

## 9. Build each other up, don't tear each other down.
Do not attempt to expose the petty faults of others, such as through shaming, criticizing, or arguing. This is not a platform for debating, trying to control others, or even change their minds.

## 10. Remember, there are kids in the room.
Keeping minors out of our space is not really possible, and enforcing ID verification comes with some really terrible privacy and safety concerns, so always assume there are minors present and behave accordingly.

# __Community Guidelines__
## Beginning Anew (Conflict Resolution)
It's okay if we don't all agree. We can offer others a chance to understand our perspective, but we cannot force them to agree.

It's okay if we find each other frustrating.  We can look at our own tendency towards frustration, and we can come together with the other person to start over by [Beginning Anew](https://www.lionsroar.com/beginning-anew/).

The rules are not meant to create a community that is allergic to conflict, but rather to create the conditions where potential conflicts can be handled with compassion and understanding.

# __Stewardship__
Every member is a steward of the community. Each of us contributes to supporting a healthy and wholesome place for practice by engaging in good Dharma-friendship.

The members who have volunteered for the role of Steward are simply here to promote safety and support the conditions for shared Dharma practice.

The Stewardship role is not a position of authority, but one of service.

While we make every effort to de-escalate any situation, sometimes the Stewards need to take action for the sake of the Sangha.

The Stewards are guided to respond in the following ways:

## 1. Encourage Responsibility
Remind the community that even if a person seems like a troublemaker, we can still treat them with respect and compassion while we investigate or try to clear up any misunderstanding.

## 2. Mindfully Clarify
Sometimes there is a miscommunication, misunderstanding, or a mistaken expectation, and all we need to do is clarify with the other person what is being said, what is being meant, and what is expected in this community.

## 3. Provide Space
A "timeout" can be useful when a situation needs to be resolved while there is too much agitation in the community. Stewards may issue a timeout and ask to speak privately with someone who needs help.

## 4. Invite Collaboration
A Steward may ask the other person, members of the community, or other Stewards for insight and perspective when dealing with a difficult situation.

## 5. Set Boundaries
When nothing else works, a Steward may issue a ban so that folks whose behavior is not aligned with the intentions of the Virtual Sangha can find what they're looking for somewhere else.

**Stewards may make mistakes.**
Stewards will always offer multiple invitations to reconcile or issue timeouts, and will typically consult with other Stewards before making any final decisions.

If, however, you feel a Steward is making an error, feel free to open a ticket in <#1509231264379699200>  or send a message to an Admin.

# Founding Scriptures
*"Intention, I tell you, is kamma."* - The Buddha, AN 6.63

*"Good friends, companions, and associates are the whole of the spiritual life."* - The Buddha, SN 3.18

*"Monks, I do not see a single thing that gives rise to unskillful qualities, or, when they have arisen, makes them increase and grow like wrong view. When you have wrong view, unskillful qualities arise or, when they have arisen, they increase and grow.*

*"Monks, I do not see a single thing that gives rise to skillful qualities, or, when they have arisen, makes them increase and grow like right view. When you have right view, unarisen skillful qualities arise or, when they have arisen, they increase and grow.* - The Budhha, AN 1.306

In MN 9,  Sariputta says: A noble disciple has right view when they understand what is skillful, and the root of skillfulness, and they understand what is unskillful, and the root of unskilfulness.

And what is unskillful? Breaking the 5 precepts.

What is the root of unskillfulness? The Three Poisons: greed, hate, and delusion.

What is skillful? Keeping the 5 precepts.

What is the root of skillfulness? Contentment, love, and understanding.

The monks ask, is there another way of describing right view?

Sariputta answers: *A noble disciple has right view when they understand the Four Noble Truths.*

In MN 95 and DN 31, The Buddha gives instructions to lay people on how to cultivate their practice, including guidelines on ethical conduct as well as the importance of seeking out a teacher, finding opportunities to hear the Dharma, and generating confidence in the teachings.

These suttas listed above form the constitution of the Virtual Sangha.

Our intention is to cultivate good spiritual friendship, where we support each other in connecting with and growing confidence in the teachings, to understand the Four Noble Truths and to apply the Five Precepts to our lives.

We continue to nurture the growth and development of ourselves and our friends here, while also networking with reputable teachers and monastics more broadly.

The internet is full of debate-bros, misinformation, and scams, and we don't have much or any tolerance for those things here.

Providing a place where people can show up with any level of familiarity, get connected with wholesome friendship from lay and monastic disciples, take refuge, and even deepen their study and practice by enrolling in programs from the Dharma Teacher Order is the fillfillment of those intentions, and it has been an honor to see those intentions bear fruit.
